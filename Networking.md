# Hackathon Networking Playbook

**Me:** Syed
**Goal:** Make a genuine, memorable connection and leave with a LinkedIn connection.

---

- [ ] Fill in your project in the intros below: [project] = ________, [what it does / who it helps] = ________
- [ ] Practice the intro out loud twice
- [ ] Pick 2 topics per person you're most curious about
- [ ] Have your LinkedIn QR code open on your phone

---

## The Conversation (2–5 minutes)

**1. Introduce yourself (~15 sec):** Wait for a natural pause, make eye contact, shake hands, and say your name clearly. Use the intro on the person's card below.

**2. Break the ice:**
- "What brought you out here? Judging, mentoring, or scouting talent?"
- "Have you seen any projects so far that stood out?"
- "What would make a hackathon project interesting to someone at [company]?"

**3. Talk tech:** Pick 1–2 questions from their card. If they mention something newer, ask them to explain it.

**4. Career advice:**
- "How did you get into [industry] technology?"
- "What problem are you most excited about right now?"
- "What skills do you wish more new engineers had?"
- "What surprised you about working at [company]'s scale?"
- "If you were starting your career today, what would you focus on?"

**5. Pitch your project (optional):**
> "We're building [project], which [what it does] for [who it helps]. If you were judging, what would you push us on?"

Asking for feedback makes you memorable.

**6. Wrap up:**
> "Thanks so much for your time, this was really helpful. Would you mind if I connected with you on LinkedIn?"

If they seem busy:
> "I'll let you go, but could I connect with you on LinkedIn?"

**7. Follow up within 24 hours:**
> Hi [Name], this is Syed. We met at [hackathon name] today and talked about [topic]. I really appreciated your insight on [something they said]. I'd love to stay connected and keep learning from your work at [company]. Thanks again!

---

## Do's and Don'ts

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Listen more than you talk | Ask for a job or referral right away |
| Follow what they get excited about | Talk only about yourself |
| Keep it to 2–5 minutes | Overstay if they're trying to move on |
| Be curious and genuine | Recite a rehearsed pitch |

## Person 3: SAP Workshop

**Host / speaker:** David Nolan, SAP National Security Services (NS2)  **Workshop topic:** ________

**Workshop tips:**
- Sit near the front and take notes on 1–2 things David says that you want to ask about.
- Ask one thoughtful question during Q&A so he recognizes you afterward.
- Approach right after it ends, before the crowd forms, or wait for the line to clear.

**Intro (after the workshop):**
> "Hi David, I'm Syed. I really enjoyed the part about [something from the workshop]. I'm building [project] this weekend, and I'm curious how you'd think about it if a government or highly regulated customer wanted to use it."

**Casual intro:**
> "Hi, I'm Syed. I didn't know much about NS2 before today. What's the biggest difference between building for national security customers and regular enterprise?"

**Tech topics:**

| Topic | Ask |
|---|---|
| **Secure / sovereign cloud** | "What changes when you run cloud software for customers whose data can't leave strict boundaries?" |
| **AI in regulated environments** | "How do you bring AI tools like Joule to customers who can't send data to public models?" |
| **Compliance (FedRAMP, DoD levels)** | "How much does compliance shape the way your engineers design and ship software?" |
| **Supply chain & logistics** | "Where does SAP software make the biggest difference for defense or government logistics?" |
| **Data & integration** | "How do you connect legacy government systems that were never designed to talk to each other?" |
| **Career path** | "What should a student know if they're interested in tech work in the national security space?" |
| **Why a separate company** | "Why does SAP run NS2 as its own company instead of just a division?" |
| **Insider threat & zero trust** | "How do you design systems assuming someone inside might be a threat?" |
| **Air-gapped & classified systems** | "How do you deliver updates or new features to systems that aren't connected to the internet?" |
| **Defense readiness** | "How does software help the military know if equipment and supplies are ready when needed?" |
| **Moving fast vs. staying secure** | "Government is known for moving slowly. How do you ship modern software without cutting corners on security?" |
| **AI trust & explainability** | "What does a defense or intelligence customer need before they'll trust an AI's recommendation?" |
| **Commercial vs. government tech** | "Do you take commercial SAP products and lock them down, or build things specifically for government?" |
| **Day-to-day work** | "What does a normal week look like for an engineer at NS2?" |
| **Getting started** | "Are there internships or early-career paths at NS2, and what makes a candidate stand out?" |

**Tip:** If he mentions security clearances or citizenship requirements, that's a natural opening for the career question, since NS2 roles often have those requirements.
